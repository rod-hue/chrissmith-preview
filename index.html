The man behind the effect

A strategist who learned the work door by door.

Chris Smith is a nationally experienced political strategist, campaign advisor, organizing innovator, labor advocate and author. His work spans consequential presidential, gubernatorial, congressional and statewide campaigns.

Over those campaigns he built a distinctive grassroots model: local leadership, relationship-based organizing, coalition engagement and disciplined voter mobilization. It has held up in very different political environments, from a Mississippi special election to a national presidential primary.

Today Chris leads electoral and advocacy work for the ACLU of Florida, bringing electoral strategy, statewide advocacy and coalition building to the defense of fundamental freedoms and democratic participation.

At March On he served as National Campaigns Director, leading a national voting rights campaign. He was Deputy National Field Director for Bernie Sanders in 2016, Deputy Campaign Manager for Stacey Abrams in 2018 and a Southern State Director for Amy Klobuchar in 2020. At SEIU he served as State Power Director in Virginia.

How the effect works

A community-centered model for building mobilization at scale.

The model connects national or statewide goals to local relationships, trusted community leadership, volunteer development and coordinated field execution. It builds lasting capacity instead of chasing short-term contacts. Six moves, repeated in every race.

  1. 01

    Trusted messengers

    Organizing starts with the people a community already listens to. Relationships first, scripts second.

  2. 02

    Local leaders

    Recruit, train and hand real ownership to local leaders, so the work outlives the staff who seeded it.

  3. 03

    Coalitions

    Bring churches, unions, campuses, Greek letter organizations and small businesses into one plan.

  4. 04

    Volunteer to voter

    A system that turns a volunteer into a team captain and a team captain into hundreds of voter contacts.

  5. 05

    Field meets strategy

    Field operations wired directly to campaign objectives, with data deciding where the next hour goes.

  6. 06

    Built to last

    Infrastructure that is still standing the morning after Election Day, ready for the next fight.

The effect at scale · March On

Ninety-five cities. One day.

On the anniversary of the March on Washington, Chris coordinated a national day of action that put organizers on the ground in 95 cities at once. The same team took the Vote With Us bus tour through the South in 2020, then stayed in Georgia through the Senate runoffs.

The culture effect

Where culture meets the ballot box.

Chris founded Vote Influencers to connect campaigns and nonprofits with entertainers and creators who move people. The work has brought Killer Mike, Patti LaBelle, Ray J and DJ A-One to campuses and town halls, and it powers the annual HBCU Achiever Awards during Congressional Black Caucus week in Washington.

The effect draws a crowd

The company he keeps.

When Chris calls, artists, legends and leaders show up, and so do the voters who follow them. Culture is part of the organizing model, not decoration on top of it.
